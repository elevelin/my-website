Behind the Scenes of My Creative Process

Published on September 24, 2026 by Amber Rasmussen

Most ideas don't arrive finished. Mine usually arrive as a mess.

A random thought. A sketch. A problem that bothers me enough to want to fix it. A hairstyle I want to try. A website that could work better. A technical problem that makes me think, there has to be a better way to do this.

That's usually where the process starts.

I've never really been someone who works inside one creative box. My interests bounce between art, technology, barbering, design, and whatever else happens to catch my attention. On the surface, those things might seem completely unrelated. To me, they're all versions of the same thing:

Taking an idea and turning it into something real.

Start With Curiosity

I don't always start with a plan.

Sometimes I'll see something and immediately want to know how it was made. Sometimes I'll have an idea for something that doesn't exist yet. Other times, someone has a problem and I start thinking about how I could make it easier.

Curiosity tends to be the first step.

I'll research, experiment, take things apart, put them back together, and occasionally make something much more complicated than it needed to be.

But that's part of the fun.

Make It, Then Make It Better

I'm a big believer in actually making things instead of waiting until everything is perfect.

The first version might be rough. A drawing might change halfway through. A website might get redesigned three times. A technical project might work perfectly until I discover one completely ridiculous problem hiding somewhere in the middle.

That's when the real work starts.

I test things. I change things. I break things. I figure out why they broke.

Then I try again.

The finished product is rarely what I imagined when I started, and honestly, that's usually a good thing.

Where Barbering Fits In

Barbering has become another outlet for that same creative process.

There's technique involved, obviously, but there's also observation and problem-solving. Every head of hair is different. Every person has different features, preferences, textures, and ideas about what they want.

A haircut isn't something you can completely copy and paste from one person to another.

You have to look at what's in front of you and figure out what works.

That's the part I love.

Art Without Rules

Art gives me a different kind of freedom.

There's no troubleshooting guide telling you whether an idea is supposed to work. Sometimes you just make something because you like the way it looks or because you want to see what happens.

I don't think everything needs a deeper meaning.

Sometimes creating something simply because you can is enough.

Technology Is Its Own Kind of Creativity

Technology might seem like the opposite of art, but I've never experienced it that way.

Writing code, building websites, automating tasks, troubleshooting systems, and designing digital projects all require creativity. There's usually more than one way to solve a problem, and sometimes the most interesting solution is the one nobody thought to try yet.

That's what keeps me interested.

I'm not just interested in using technology. I like understanding it, modifying it, and figuring out what else it can do.

The Part That Matters Most

There's one thing that connects all of these interests for me: I like being useful.

I have a servant spirit. If I can create something that makes someone's life easier, solve a problem, teach someone something, give them a look they feel good wearing, or simply make their day a little better, that's meaningful to me.

Creativity isn't always about making something impressive.

Sometimes it's about making something useful.

Sometimes it's about making something beautiful.

Sometimes it's about making something that didn't exist yesterday.

And sometimes it's about helping someone else make something of their own.

That's the process behind what I do.

It's messy. It's experimental. It's constantly changing.

And I'm pretty sure that's exactly how I want it.
